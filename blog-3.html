The Perfect Wine Pairing for a Wood-Fired Dinner — A Beginner's Guide

June 18, 2026 · 6 min read · By Sofia Laurent, Sommelier

Wine pairing intimidates people more than it should. The rules are simpler than they seem — and when it comes to wood-fired cuisine specifically, the guiding principle is beautifully straightforward: match the intensity. Big, smoky, charred flavours need wines with enough character to hold their own. Delicate wines get lost. Bold wines soar.

As the sommelier at Ember & Thyme, I've spent years refining which bottles work best with our kitchen's signature fire-driven dishes. Here's what I've learned.

Understanding Wood-Fired Flavour First

Before we talk wine, we need to talk about what the fire actually does to food. Wood-fired cooking creates three distinct flavour layers that you won't find in conventional cooking:

  • Char — the slightly bitter, caramelised crust that forms on direct-heat proteins and vegetables
  • Smoke — a deep, aromatic earthiness that penetrates the outer layers of whatever is being cooked
  • Sweetness — the natural sugars in meat and vegetables caramelise at high heat, creating a subtle sweetness beneath the char

A great wine pairing acknowledges all three layers — complementing the char, harmonising with the smoke, and letting the sweetness breathe.

"The best wine pairing doesn't overpower the food or disappear beneath it. It creates a third flavour — one that only exists when the two are together."

Our Signature Pairings at Ember & Thyme

Wood-Fired Pizza

Pairs with: Primitivo or Zinfandel

The jammy, spiced fruit of a good Primitivo mirrors the sweetness of slow-cooked tomato and stands up beautifully to the char on the crust. Look for bottles from Puglia, Italy — they're made for exactly this kind of food.

Thyme Butter Salmon

Pairs with: White Burgundy (Chardonnay)

A well-aged Burgundy — creamy, with notes of toasted oak and lemon curd — is the classic companion to rich, butter-basted salmon. The acidity cuts through the fat while the oak echoes the smoke. If Burgundy is out of budget, look for a good Willamette Valley Chardonnay from Oregon.

Handmade Pasta with Brown Butter

Pairs with: Barolo or Barbaresco

These powerful Piedmontese reds — made from the Nebbiolo grape — have the tannin structure and earthy complexity to match pasta's richness without crushing it. The dried rose and tar notes in a good Barolo turn a great pasta dish into an exceptional one.

The Rule That Overrides All Other Rules

Here's the truth about wine pairing that every sommelier eventually admits: the best pairing is the one you enjoy. Rules about red with meat and white with fish exist for good reasons, but they're starting points, not commandments.

If you love a particular wine, tell your server. We'll work backwards from there to find dishes that will make that bottle shine. That's what we're here for — not to lecture you about terroir, but to make sure your evening is exactly what you wanted it to be.

A Note on Our Wine List

We curate our wine list with wood-fired cuisine specifically in mind. Every bottle has been tasted alongside our dishes before it earns a place on the menu. When you dine with us, ask for a pairing recommendation — we love this part of the job, and we'll never steer you wrong.
